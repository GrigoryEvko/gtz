\section{Second routes}\label{app:routes}

Several facts of the body admit more than one proof.  The body carries one
of them --- the shortest, or the one that also classifies the equality
case, or the one that runs over both fields at once --- and this appendix
carries the others.  Nothing here is needed downstream.  Each subsection
names the body statement it reaches and says what it gives up in
reaching it.

\subsection{The maximal-volume bound}\label{app:volumebound}

This is the derivation announced in \S\ref{sec:intro}: the classical
argument at uniform weights, in full.  It reaches the range
\eqref{eq:elementary} and no further, whereas \S\ref{sec:first} and
\S\ref{sec:chart} settle that range outright and classify its equality
case.  Problem~\ref{prob:effective} asks for the best constant the
argument can be made to give, so the constant is tracked here at every
step.  Compare \S\ref{app:volume}, where the same principle is used to
\emph{name} a subset and the resulting rule is false.

Let $U\in\FF^{m\times k}$ satisfy $U\adj U=I_k$ and let $u_c\adj$ be its
$c$-th row.  Among the finitely many $k$-subsets choose one of maximal
volume, that is one maximizing $\lvert\det U_C\rvert$.  It is nonsingular:
by the Cauchy--Binet formula \cite[\S0.8.7]{HornJohnson2013}
$\sum_{\lvert C\rvert=k}\lvert\det U_C\rvert^2=\det(U\adj U)=1$, and a
finite family of nonnegative numbers summing to $1$ has a positive
maximum.  Put $B:=UU_C^{-1}$, the matrix expressing each row of $U$ in the
basis of the selected rows.  Replacing the $j$-th row of $U_C$ by the
$r$-th row of $U$ replaces that row by its expansion $\sum_i B_{ri}(U_C)_i$
in the selected rows, since $BU_C=U$.  Expand the determinant linearly in
that row.  For $i\ne j$ the term carries the row $(U_C)_i$ in position $i$
and again in position $j$, so it vanishes; only $i=j$ survives, and the new
determinant is $B_{rj}\det U_C$.  For $r\notin C$ the matrix so formed
carries, in some order, the rows of $U$ on the set obtained from $C$ by
replacing its $j$-th index with $r$.  That set again has $k$ elements,
since $r\notin C$, so an entry $B_{rj}$ of modulus greater than $1$ would
name a $k$-subset of strictly larger volume.  Maximality therefore bounds
$\lvert B_{rj}\rvert$ by $1$ at every $j$ and every $r\notin C$.  The
remaining rows, those indexed by $C$, are $U_CU_C^{-1}=I_k$, since
restriction to the rows $C$ commutes with multiplication on the right.
The two cases exhaust the rows, so every entry of $B$ has modulus at most
$1$.  Those $k$ rows contribute exactly $k$ to the squared Frobenius norm,
and each of the other $m-k$ rows carries $k$ entries of modulus at most
$1$ and so contributes at most $k$, whence
\[
  \tr\bigl((U_C\adj U_C^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}})^{-1}\bigr)
  \;=\;\lVert U_C^{-1}\rVert_F^2
  \;=\;\lVert B\rVert_F^2
  \;\le\;k+k(m-k),
\]
using $(U_C\adj U_C^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}})^{-1}=U_C^{-1}(U_C^{-1})\adj$ at
the first equality and $\lVert UX\rVert_F=\lVert X\rVert_F$ at the second.
The row identity at $U$ and again at $U_C$ gives
$I_k-U_C\adj U_C^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}
=\sum_{c\notin C}u_c^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}u_c\adj\psd0$.  Inversion
reverses the Loewner order between positive definite matrices, and
$U_C\adj U_C^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}$ is one because $U_C$ is nonsingular,
so $(U_C\adj U_C^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}})^{-1}\psd I_k$.  A Hermitian
$k\times k$ matrix dominating the identity has all its eigenvalues at least
$1$.  Its largest eigenvalue is the trace less the other $k-1$, each of
which is at least $1$, so it is at most the trace diminished by $k-1$.
Therefore $\lVert U_C^{-1}\rVert_2^2
=\lmax\bigl((U_C\adj U_C^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}})^{-1}\bigr)
\le k+k(m-k)-(k-1)=k(m-k)+1$, and the two readings of a smallest singular
value, $\lVert U_C^{-1}\rVert_2=\sigma_{\min}(U_C)^{-1}$ and
$\sigma_{\min}(U_C)^2=\lmin(U_C\adj U_C^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}})$, carry
the bound back to the subset sum:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:volumebound}
  \SC\;=\;m\,U_C\adj U_C^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}
  \;\psd\;\frac{m}{k(m-k)+1}\,I_k .
\end{equation}

Two remarks on what the argument does and does not use.  The entrywise
bound $\lvert B_{rj}\rvert\le1$ is the only place maximality enters, and it
is used $k(m-k)$ times, once per off-block entry; any strengthening of the
constant must improve that count, since the rest is exact.  And the
argument never touches the weights: it is a statement about $U$ alone, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:classical} is what carries it to general weights, at
the cost of the transfer.  Proposition~\ref{prop:rankfloor} runs the same
argument again over $\FF$, in the weighted normalization and with the
conclusion read as a lower bound on the value.
