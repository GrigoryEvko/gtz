\section{The equality locus at rank two}\label{app:planar}

At uniform weights the locus is classified in \cite{Nesterenko2026real},
quoted in \S\ref{sec:ranktwo}: the squared vectors fall into three
clusters.  The arbitrary-weight classification runs through the
\emph{tight graph}, whose vertices are the atoms and whose edges are the
pairs at equality in Lemma~\ref{lem:planarsilence}.

Every declaration named here is over $\R$ at $k=2$, and the design is
all-heavy ($\ell_c>1$ at every index), which by
Corollary~\ref{cor:heavy} is the only case that matters.  The squared
vectors $\eta_c$ of \eqref{eq:doubleangle} are written in polar form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:planarcoords}
  u_c\;:=\;\eta_c/\ell_c ,
  \qquad
  \nu_c\;:=\;1-\frac1{\ell_c} ,
\end{equation}
so $u_c$ is a unit vector and the \emph{conic value} $\nu_c$ lies in
$(0,1)$ and increases with the leverage.

\subsection{Silence, tightness, and the focal conic}\label{app:planardict}

\begin{lemma}[Lean]\label{lem:planarsilence}
A pair of heavy atoms dominates if and only if
$\langle g_c,g_d\rangle^2\le(\ell_c-1)(\ell_d-1)$; in the coordinates
\eqref{eq:planarcoords} this reads
$\tfrac12\bigl(1+\langle u_c,u_d\rangle\bigr)\le\nu_c\nu_d$.
\end{lemma}

The $2\times2$ excess $\eta_c\eta_c\tp+\eta_d\eta_d\tp-I$ has trace
$\ell_c+\ell_d-2$, positive on heavy atoms, so its positive
semidefiniteness is the sign of its determinant, and that determinant is
$(\ell_c-1)(\ell_d-1)-\langle g_c,g_d\rangle^2$.  The second form is the
first divided by $\ell_c\ell_d$, using that squaring doubles arguments.

A pair is \emph{tight} at equality and \emph{silent} when the inequality
runs the other way.

Where no pair dominates, the multipliers of a stationary system place
every atom on a conic.  Pairing that conic against $\sum_ct_c=1$,
$\sum_ct_c\eta_c=0$ and $\sum_ct_c\ell_c=2$ eliminates its free constant.

\begin{proposition}[Lean]\label{prop:focalconic}
Atoms on the conic $\mu_0+\langle\xi,\eta_c\rangle+\beta\ell_c=0$ satisfy
$\mu_0=-2\beta$, and dividing by the leverage puts every atom on
\begin{equation}\label{eq:focalconic}
  \nu_c\;=\;\tfrac12+\langle\tilde\xi,u_c\rangle ,
  \qquad \tilde\xi:=-\xi/(2\beta) .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Antipodal directions pair to one, $\nu(-u)=1-\nu(u)$, the reflection
sending leverage $\ell$ to its conjugate $\ell/(\ell-1)$.  Positivity of
the conic at every direction is finiteness and positivity of every
direction's leverage, and evaluating at the antipode of $\tilde\xi$'s own
direction turns it into $\lVert\tilde\xi\rVert<\tfrac12$.

The covector exists at all:

\begin{proposition}[Lean]\label{prop:momentcovector}
At a triangle of pairwise-tight atoms of leverage above one there is a
$\tilde\xi$ satisfying \eqref{eq:focalconic} at all three, and the conic
values satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spread}
  2\nu_a\nu_b\nu_c+\nu_a\nu_b+\nu_a\nu_c+\nu_b\nu_c-1\;=\;0 .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Eliminating the geometry from the three tightness relations leaves a
product of \eqref{eq:spread} with
$2\nu_a\nu_b\nu_c-\nu_a\nu_b-\nu_a\nu_c-\nu_b\nu_c+1$, exactly one factor
of which vanishes.  In the co-leverage coordinates $x=1-\nu=1/\ell$ the
second factor is $xy(1-z)+xz(1-y)+yz$, and multiplied by
$\ell_a\ell_b\ell_c$ it is $\ell_a+\ell_b+\ell_c-2$: positive as soon as
the three leverages pass two, so heavy atoms cannot satisfy it and
\eqref{eq:spread} is forced.  The informal argument runs in the angle
parameterization $p+q+r=\pi$, which assumes the surviving branch rather
than selecting it.

Equation \eqref{eq:spread} is the weight-free form of the cluster law.
Write $\tau_a,\tau_b,\tau_c$ for the total weights of the three clusters
of \S\ref{sec:ranktwo}.  Then $\nu_X=(1-\tau_X)/(1+\tau_X)$ with
$\tau_a+\tau_b+\tau_c=1$ satisfies \eqref{eq:spread} identically, and at
uniform weights $\tau_X$ is the cluster's size over $m$.

\subsection{The tight graph has maximum degree two}\label{app:tightgraph}

Fix an atom and read \eqref{eq:focalconic} into
Lemma~\ref{lem:planarsilence}.  Tightness against that atom becomes a
condition $\langle n,u\rangle+e=0$ on the partner's direction, with $n$
and $e$ depending only on the fixed atom and the covector.  The condition
is affine, and a line meets the unit circle at most twice.

\begin{theorem}[Lean]\label{thm:degreetwo}
Let a design carry the focal conic \eqref{eq:focalconic} with
$\lVert\tilde\xi\rVert<\tfrac12$, and let an atom of conic value in
$[0,1]$ be tight against three unit directions.  Then two of the three
coincide.
\end{theorem}

The tight graph is therefore a disjoint union of paths and cycles, and the
star $K_{1,3}$ does not occur in it.

\subsection{Paths and partial cycles are empty}\label{app:paths}

A \emph{stress leaf} is an atom carrying a single stressed edge.  Its
stress equation is the angular criticality of that edge's polar form, and
criticality with tightness makes the neighbour's polar line tangent to the
circle at the leaf.

\begin{theorem}[Lean]\label{thm:leaftangency}
At a stress leaf whose neighbour has conic value $\nu_j\ne1$, any
direction tight against that neighbour is the leaf's own.
\end{theorem}

A tangent line meets the circle once, so the neighbour has no second
partner and the component cannot extend.  Every path component and every
partially stressed cycle is empty.  At three atoms the remaining case is
settled by an exact factorization.

\begin{proposition}[Lean]\label{prop:pthree}
Place three atoms at directions of argument $2a$, $0$, $-2b$ with middle
conic value $n$, tight along the two edges of the path.  The four design
equations in three weights are compatible only where
$4n\sin(a+b)\cdot G$ vanishes, $G:=n\cos(a+b)+\cos a\cos b$; and the third
pair's tightness gap is $\bigl(n\cos(a+b)-\cos a\cos b\bigr)G$.
\end{proposition}

Away from clones ($\sin(a+b)\ne0$) and degenerate levels, feasibility
forces $G=0$, and $G=0$ annihilates the third gap.

The path closes into the triangle.

\subsection{No dust, and no collinear designs}\label{app:nodust}

Tightness forces $\langle g_c,g_d\rangle^2=(\ell_c-1)(\ell_d-1)$, whose
right side is negative when one leverage is below one and the other above.
A pair of atoms both below one violates the trace floor
$\ell_c+\ell_d\ge2$.  A tight pair cannot be completed by light atoms
either: if its mass parameters satisfy the closure bound
$\alpha\beta\le(1-a)(1-b)$ and the trace bound $\alpha+\beta\ge1$ with
$\alpha\le a<1$ and $\beta\le b<1$, the chain
\[
  \alpha\beta\;\le\;(1-a)(1-b)\;\le\;(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)
  \;=\;1-\alpha-\beta+\alpha\beta\;\le\;\alpha\beta
\]
collapses and forces $\nu_A=\nu_B=1$, which no finite leverage attains.

The degenerate stratum is empty for a separate reason.

\begin{proposition}[Lean]\label{prop:collinear}
Signed positions on a line with positive weights summing to one, closure
$\sum_ct_cs_c=0$, trace $\sum_ct_c\lvert s_c\rvert=2$, and silence across
the origin, every opposite pair having a side of leverage at most one, are
contradictory.
\end{proposition}

Closure splits the leverage mass evenly, each side carrying exactly one,
and a side capped at leverage one cannot carry mass one on weight below
one.  So $\beta\ne0$ in the stationary system, and \eqref{eq:focalconic}
is available.

\subsection{The triangle is a family split}\label{app:planarcomplete}

\begin{theorem}[Lean]\label{thm:planarcomplete}
Let a design carry a pairwise-tight triangle of clusters with spanning
directions $v_A,v_B,v_C$, conic values in $(0,1)$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:focalconic}, leverages $\ell_X(1-\nu_X)=1$, and
$\lVert\tilde\xi\rVert<\tfrac12$.  If every atom is silent against all
three clusters, then every atom's squared vector is one of
$\ell_Av_A$, $\ell_Bv_B$, $\ell_Cv_C$.
\end{theorem}

The defect $d(\eta):=1-\ell\bigl(\tfrac12-\langle\tilde\xi,u\rangle\bigr)$
is nonnegative at every atom and zero only at a cluster.  Light atoms are
positive because the conic curve stays positive inside the disk
$\lVert\tilde\xi\rVert<\tfrac12$.  Heavy atoms on a cluster direction are
squeezed to that cluster's leverage by silence against a second cluster.
Heavy atoms off the cluster directions are positive because the three
chord functions
\[
  f_X(u)\;=\;\tfrac12\bigl(1+\langle v_X,u\rangle\bigr)
  -\nu_X\bigl(\tfrac12+\langle\tilde\xi,u\rangle\bigr)
\]
cannot all be nonnegative away from a vertex: tightness and
\eqref{eq:focalconic} make $f_X$ vanish at the other two cluster
directions and equal $1-\nu_X^2$ at its own, so the normalized values are
barycentric coordinates, and a unit convex combination of three distinct
unit vectors is one of them.  Pairing the three design equations against
$(1,\tilde\xi,-\tfrac12)$ gives $\sum_ct_cd_c=0$, and positive weights
annihilate the population off the clusters.

The hypotheses are satisfiable.  At the symmetric point \eqref{eq:spread}
factors as $(t+1)^2(2t-1)$, so $\nu=\tfrac12$, hence $\ell=2$ and
$\tfrac12(1+\langle v_X,v_Y\rangle)=\tfrac14$: three unit directions at
mutual angle $2\pi/3$, weights $\tfrac13$, and $\tilde\xi=0$.  That is the
rank-two trine of Example~\ref{ex:trine2}, where the theorem excludes a
fourth direction.

\subsection{What is formal}\label{app:planarformal}

Table~\ref{tab:leanplanar} names the declarations, and three
qualifications belong with them on the terms of \S\ref{sec:boundaries}.

The chain is not assembled.  Proposition~\ref{prop:collinear} and
Theorems~\ref{thm:degreetwo}--\ref{thm:planarcomplete} are formal as
stated, and their composition into a single classification is not, so
``the equality locus at rank two is a family split'' is not a statement of
the development.

The covering used in \S\ref{app:nodust}, that every atom of an equality
design lies in a tight pair, is carried by \texttt{Gtz.covered\_of\_conic}
as a per-atom contradiction: an atom of positive weight and finite
positive leverage on \eqref{eq:focalconic} cannot have an empty stress
equation, since that equation pins its direction to $2\tilde\xi$ and unit
length then forces $\lVert\tilde\xi\rVert=\tfrac12$, a conic value of one.
The quantified form over all atoms is not assembled from it.

\texttt{Gtz.dust\_pair\_never\_tight} concludes from $\ell_c<1$,
$\ell_d<1$ and $\ell_c+\ell_d\ge2$ alone; the pairing does not enter, so
the light-pair sentence of \S\ref{app:nodust} is formal only in that
weaker form.
