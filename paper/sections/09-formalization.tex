\section{Formalization}\label{sec:formalization}

The development is carried out in Lean~4 over Mathlib.
Tables~\ref{tab:lean} and~\ref{tab:leantwo} name the declaration behind
every tagged statement, and all of them where a statement is assembled from
several.  Twenty-one tags are not discharged in full, and
\S\ref{sec:boundaries} records which.  Declarations named in running text
rather than in the tables --- in \S\ref{sec:boundaries} itself and in
\S\ref{sec:open} --- back prose that carries no tag; they are covered by
the ledger of \S\ref{sec:axioms} on the same terms as the tabulated ones.

The formal statements are the originals, and the statements in the text
are their transcriptions.

\subsection{What is formalized}\label{sec:whatformal}

The formal definitions are those of \S\ref{sec:first} verbatim.  A design is a
structure carrying the vectors, the weights, positivity of the weights, their
normalization, and \eqref{eq:parseval}; domination of a subset is positive
semidefiniteness of $\SC-I$; and $\Gtz(m,k)$ is the assertion that every
design of size $m$ and rank $k$ admits a dominating subset of cardinality $k$.
No nondegeneracy is assumed: repeated atoms and zero atoms are admitted, and
the results of \S\ref{sec:ties} depend on that.

The design structure and part of the elementary theory are written once for
scalars in an \texttt{RCLike} field with real weights, and $\R$ and $\C$ are
instantiations, definitionally so over $\R$; the chart of \S\ref{sec:chart}
with its congruence, its criterion and its two Rayleigh halves,
Theorem~\ref{thm:corankone} and Proposition~\ref{prop:sizerank} are proved at
that generality and used at both.  Outside that layer the real and complex
statements are separate developments joined by transport lemmas, and
\S\ref{sec:boundaries} lists the statements printed over both fields that only
the real one reaches.  Where a statement is genuinely
field-dependent, as Theorem~\ref{thm:complex} and Theorem~\ref{thm:ranktwo}
are, it is proved at the field where it holds.

The chart of \S\ref{sec:chart} is formalized as the projection $P=VV\adj$ of
\eqref{eq:chart} together with the equivalence of Lemma~\ref{lem:chart}, and
the compactification of \S\ref{sec:compactness} as the predicate of
Definition~\ref{def:chartpoint} on pairs $(P,t)$ with $P$ a symmetric
idempotent of trace $k$ and $t$ in the closed simplex.  The stationarity
system of \S\ref{sec:interior} is a structure bundling
\eqref{eq:statg}--\eqref{eq:statt} with the active family and the
multipliers, and the results of that section are implications from it.

Graphic designs, used for the diamond of Example~\ref{ex:diamond}, are
formalized through \eqref{eq:graphicatom}.  The whitener is then an
existential and the atoms are determined only up to the orthogonal group,
so what is rational, and what the formal statements quantify over, is the
graph data.

\subsection{Axiom discipline}\label{sec:axioms}

The ledger module \texttt{Gtz/Audit.lean} lists, declaration by
declaration, the axioms on which each depends, printed by the kernel rather
than asserted by the author: $3638$ invocations of \texttt{\#print axioms}
over $153$ imported modules.  The permitted set is Mathlib's
$\{\texttt{propext},\ \texttt{Classical.choice},\ \texttt{Quot.sound}\}$, and
nothing outside it occurs.  Classical logic is used freely, and nothing in
the development is a constructive claim.

In particular no declaration depends on \texttt{sorryAx}: the development
contains no incomplete proof.  It contains no \texttt{axiom} declaration.
Closed arithmetic over concrete finite index sets is discharged by kernel
evaluation of decidable propositions, which introduces no axiom.  No proof
is closed by compiled evaluation.

The ledger covers exactly what it lists.  Of the declarations named in
Tables~\ref{tab:lean} and~\ref{tab:leantwo} one is absent from it, the entry
marked $a$ in Table~\ref{tab:leantwo}.  Its proof is checked when its module
is elaborated, but its axiom use is not certified by the ledger.

No numerical assertion in the paper rests on floating-point evaluation.  The
witnesses of \S\ref{sec:sharp}, and those of
Appendix~\ref{app:graveyard} other than the icosahedron, are exact
rationals in the formal statements and are checked as such.  The
icosahedral design --- which carries Appendix~\ref{app:graveyard}'s refutation
of the two-moment certificate as well as the data of
Appendix~\ref{app:data} --- and the graphic designs of
Appendix~\ref{app:data} carry radicals, and the
formal statements use the algebraic relations \eqref{eq:icosaconst} and
\eqref{eq:graphicleverage} that eliminate them.  Remark~\ref{rem:hygiene}
records the conditioning that forces the discipline.

\subsection{Where the formalization stops}\label{sec:boundaries}

Three steps of the argument are not formal.  All three lie in
\S\ref{sec:compactness}--\S\ref{sec:interior}.

The first is the attainment in Theorem~\ref{thm:variational}: a continuous
function on a compact space attains its minimum.  The space of chart points is
a product of a Grassmannian and a closed simplex, hence compact, and the
objective $G$ of Definition~\ref{def:chartpoint} is continuous, being a maximum
of finitely many least-eigenvalue functions of continuous matrix-valued maps.
We use this standard fact without formalizing it.  Its two nontrivial
inputs, Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary} and Corollary~\ref{cor:interiorcex},
which place the minimizer at a design rather than at a boundary point,
are formal.

The second is one half of Proposition~\ref{prop:chartdesign}.  That a design
produces a chart point is formal; that every chart point with positive
weights is the chart point of a design is left as the named leaf
\texttt{Gtz.ChartPointHasDesign}, whose content is the spectral theorem.
Theorem~\ref{thm:variational}'s conclusion that the minimizer is the chart
point of a design rides on it, and so does the identification of
$\Gtz(m,k)$ with $\Gtz^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\circ}(m,k)$ in
Corollary~\ref{cor:equivalent}.

The third is the passage from a local minimum to the system
\eqref{eq:statg}--\eqref{eq:statt}.  Its ingredients are the spectraplex,
the composition rule and the maximum rule, assembled in \S\ref{sec:kkt};
each is classical
\cite{Danskin1967,Overton1988,HiriartUrrutyYe1995,Clarke1990} and none is
in the ambient library.  So \S\ref{sec:interior} takes the system as its
hypothesis and formalizes its consequences
(Proposition~\ref{prop:quadric}, Lemma~\ref{lem:overlap},
Corollary~\ref{cor:singleactive} and Proposition~\ref{prop:saturated})
rather than deriving the system from minimality.

The two conjectural inputs of Theorem~\ref{thm:rankthree} speak about the
system, not about minimality, so a proof of either closes the corresponding
cell only together with the three steps above.

Twenty-one tags are narrower than the statement they head, in two families.

Twelve are one mechanism: the statement is printed over $\FF$, or over both
fields, and the formal statement is over $\R$.  The field-generic layer is
the design structure, the trace identity, the chart of \S\ref{sec:chart}
with its congruence and its two Rayleigh halves, the weak form of
Lemma~\ref{lem:rankone}, the co-Parseval operator,
Proposition~\ref{prop:sizerank} and Theorem~\ref{thm:corankone}.  The twelve
lie outside it: the spanning assertion of Lemma~\ref{lem:span}; the
pigeonhole half of Lemma~\ref{lem:trace}; Lemma~\ref{lem:share};
Corollary~\ref{cor:minor}; Lemma~\ref{lem:whiten}; the strict half of
Lemma~\ref{lem:rankone}; the complex half of
Proposition~\ref{prop:classical}; Theorem~\ref{thm:duality};
Corollary~\ref{cor:dualcell}; Theorem~\ref{thm:leverageaverage};
Theorem~\ref{thm:tieseverywhere} over $\C$ above size $k+1$; and
Proposition~\ref{prop:twomoment}.  In each of them the printed proof does not
consult the field, so each is a transcription rather than a mathematical
gap --- with the exception named next.

The exception is Theorem~\ref{thm:duality} and, with it,
Corollary~\ref{cor:dualcell}.  No complex Naimark duality is stated anywhere
in the development, and the complex half of the corollary would need one; it
does follow from Theorem~\ref{thm:complex}, but \S\ref{sec:chart} precedes
\S\ref{sec:complex} and that route is not available where the corollary
stands.  Even over $\R$ the mechanized duality is not the printed
construction: the paper takes $h_c=R\adj w_c$ from an orthonormal basis of
$\ker P$, while the formal proof whitens
$\sum_c(1-t_c)g_c^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}g_c\adj$ and draws the dual atoms
from an orthonormal completion.  What is mechanized is that some dual design
with the same weights flips domination, not that this one does.

Proposition~\ref{prop:rankfloor} is not among the twelve.  It is mechanized
at $\R$ and at $\C$ separately, by the same three steps, as\\
\texttt{Gtz.gtzWeightedFloor\_inv\_rank} and\\
\texttt{Gtz.complexRankConstantAtLeast\_rankInverse};\\
what is missing is only the form at a generic \texttt{RCLike} scalar.

Nine are gaps inside one field: a clause of the printed statement has no
formal counterpart at either.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\rm(i)] Theorem~\ref{thm:corankone}(i), the erasure criterion, is
  carried in the development as the named open proposition\\
  \texttt{Gtz.JensenErasureCriterion}.  What is formal is the same criterion
  in the pivot chart,\\
  \texttt{Gtz.fieldDominates\_erase\_iff\_coParsevalPivot\_le\_one},\\
  with the strict half over $\R$ only; the step between them is the
  uniqueness of rank-one Hermitian idempotents, which the ambient library
  does not package.  Parts (ii)--(iv) are formal.
\item[\rm(ii)] In Proposition~\ref{prop:tielawcorank} the per-atom
  equivalence is formal, as\\
  \texttt{Gtz.jensenRatio\_eq\_inv\_rank\_iff\_leverageIdentity},\\
  and the summation that concludes $m=k+1$ is not.
\item[\rm(iii)] In Proposition~\ref{prop:jensen} the inequality is formal, as\\
  \texttt{Gtz.sq\_rank\_le\_sum\_weight\_mul\_leverage\_sq},\\
  and the equality case, $\ell_c=k$ throughout, is not stated.
\item[\rm(iv)] In Lemma~\ref{lem:overlap} the covering asserted in~(iv) is
  formal, as\\
  \texttt{Gtz.exists\_mem\_activeSubset\_of\_isQuadricStationaryData},\\
  and the refinement that the covering index carries $\lambda_i>0$ and
  $\langle u_i,g_c\rangle\ne0$ is not.  That refinement survives only inside
  the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:e2}, whose printed argument consumes it,
  as does \eqref{eq:count}.
\item[\rm(v)] In Lemma~\ref{lem:defect} the two consequences drawn from the
  identity elsewhere in the section are formal, as\\
  \texttt{Gtz.weight\_mul\_value\_le\_one\_of\_isQuadricStationaryData} and\\
  \texttt{Gtz.weight\_mul\_value\_eq\_one\_of\_saturatedAtom};\\
  the identity itself, one subtraction of \eqref{eq:coverage} from
  \eqref{eq:overlapone}, is not stated on its own, and neither is the
  criterion for its vanishing.
\item[\rm(vi)] In Lemma~\ref{lem:sicvalue} the value is formal on one side,
  as \texttt{Gtz.sicDesign\_valueAtMost} together with the vanishing of the
  shifted pair determinant; the attainment that turns ``at most'' into
  ``exactly'' is not stated.  Example~\ref{ex:trine} and
  Proposition~\ref{prop:hesse} carry the matching greatest-element
  declarations and this one does not.
\item[\rm(vii)] In Proposition~\ref{prop:hesse} the root
  $3(1-\cos\tfrac{2\pi}9)$ is formal, as \texttt{Gtz.hesseMargin\_isRoot};
  the other two roots of $h$ do not occur in the development, and neither
  does the assertion that the first is the least of the three.  The rest of
  the proposition is formal.
\item[\rm(viii)] Theorem~\ref{thm:reduce} prints the smaller design's vectors
  indexed in increasing order, where\\
  \texttt{Gtz.exists\_reduced\_design}\\
  concludes only that the index map is injective; the enumeration is what the
  construction builds and not what the statement carries.  Nothing downstream

Two further declarations reach past what the text credits.  The mechanism of
Lemma~\ref{lem:continuity} is formal for the design objective $F$ --- Weyl's
bound as \texttt{Gtz.lipschitzWith\_lambdaMinCLM}, the finite maximum as
\texttt{Gtz.continuous\_dominationMargin} --- while the transcription to the
chart, with the additive modulus in $\lVert P-Q\rVert$ and
$\max_c\lvert t_c-t'_c\rvert$, is not stated.  And
Lemma~\ref{lem:marginal} runs the other way: the paper proves it, by

neither is an over-claim.
