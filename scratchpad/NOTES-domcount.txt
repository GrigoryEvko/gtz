## 3. THE GRAM-SUBMATRIX CRITERION  (derived 2026-08-22)

For a weighted design of rank k and a k-subset C, let X be the k x k matrix whose
columns are the atoms of C.  Then S_C = X X^T and Gram[C] = X^T X are SIMILAR,
so they have the same spectrum, and

    Dominates D C   <->   Gram(D)[C]  >=  I_k
                    <->   (Gram(D) - 1)[C]  is positive semidefinite,

the PRINCIPAL C-SUBMATRIX of the m x m matrix Gram - 1.  Strictly, > .
This is exact at |C| = k and needs no invariant computation.

Consequences.  With T = diag(t) and P := T^{1/2} Gram T^{1/2} we have
P^2 = P (Parseval), rank P = k, and

    Dominates D C   <->   P[C]  >=  T[C] .

So GtzWeighted m k is EXACTLY:

    for every rank-k orthogonal projection P on R^m and every positive
    diagonal T with trace one, some k-subset C has  P[C] >= T[C] .

Naimark: Q = 1 - P is a rank-(m-k) projection and P[C] >= T[C] iff
Q[C] <= (1-T)[C].  At (6,3) both P and Q have rank 3 -- the self-dual cell.

Cauchy-Binet: det P[C] = nu_C = t_a t_b t_c [abc]^2 and sum over the 20 triples
of det P[C] = 1 (Gtz.volumeParseval).
