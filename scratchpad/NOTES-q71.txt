       complement of the Parseval projection.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Gtz.weighted_naimark_duality builds its dual by whitening the CO-PARSEVAL
operator W = sum_c (1-t_c) g_c g_c^T, i.e. A has rows sqrt(1-t_c)*R^T g_c with

completion [A|B].  The naive construction -- complete V (rows sqrt(t_c) g_c) and
rescale by 1/sqrt(t_c) -- is a DIFFERENT design and does NOT carry the
domination correspondence: 161 mismatches out of 400 on sampled boundary

which is why the diamond passed the naive test.  With the correct construction:

BOTH weak and strict domination transfer.

F71-3  THE RANK-TWO REFORMULATION (clean, and the right place to search)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
At (5,3), m = k+2, so the dual has rank two and dominating TRIPLES correspond to
dominating PAIRS of the dual.  In the dual write u_c = h_c/|h_c| (unit),
m_c = t_c*l_c, y_c = 1 - 1/l_c.  Then

    sum_c m_c u_c u_c^T = I_2,  sum_c m_c = 2,  y_c in [0,1),  sum_c m_c y_c = 1,
    pair {a,b} dominates        <=>  y_a y_b >= <u_a,u_b>^2,
    pair {a,b} dominates strictly <=> y_a y_b >  <u_a,u_b>^2.

So a boundary system is exactly:  y_a y_b <= <u_a,u_b>^2 for ALL pairs, and the
dominating pairs are the EQUALITIES.  (Some equality is then automatic from the
landed GtzWeighted 5 2.)  Diamond dual in these coordinates, verified exactly:
    l^dual = (3, 7/4, 7/4, 7/4, 7/4),  m = (0.6, 0.35, 0.35, 0.35, 0.35),
    y = (2/3, 3/7, 3/7, 3/7, 3/7),  line angles (deg) = (0, 122.31, 57.69,
    122.31, 57.69) -- three directions at 60 degrees apart, two duplicated.
    8 tight pairs; the two slack pairs are the two duplicated (parallel) ones.

F71-4  THE PIVOT CHART AND THE HALF FLOOR  -- LANDED, no duality needed
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Write W = subsetSum D univ - 1 = sum_c (1-t_c) g_c g_c^T (PosDef,
Gtz.posDef_fullExcess), rho_a = Gtz.pivot D univ a = g_a . (W^-1 g_a), and
pi_ab = g_a . (W^-1 g_b).  Three moments:

  (i)   sum_c (1-t_c) rho_c = k            Gtz.descent_identity        [landed]
  (ii)  sum_c (1-t_c)       = m-1          Gtz.sum_one_sub_weight      [landed]
  (iii) sum_c (1-t_c) pi_ac^2 = rho_a      Gtz.sum_coWeight_mul_resolvent_reading_sq
                                                                       [NEW]
(iii) is Parseval read once in the resolvent metric and is hypothesis-free at
every size and rank.

At (5,3), (i)+(ii) give sum_c (1-t_c)(1-rho_c) = 4-3 = 1.  A boundary system
refuses every triple, a triple is a double downdate of W, so
Gtz.tie_fiveSet_pair_reading at F = univ gives for every pair
    1 <= rho_a   or   (1-rho_a)(1-rho_b) <= pi_ab^2 .
Feed that into (iii):
    rho_a = (1-t_a) rho_a^2 + sum_{c/=a} (1-t_c) pi_ac^2
         >= (1-t_a) rho_a^2 + (1-rho_a)[1 - (1-t_a)(1-rho_a)]
    ==>  t_a (2 rho_a - 1) >= 0  ==>  rho_a >= 1/2.
LANDED as Gtz.pivot_half_floor.  Equality holds iff every pair inequality at a
is an equality.

    rho_a = 1/2  <=>  all four triples avoiding a dominate

F71-5  WHAT REMAINS: Q7.1 is now ONE scalar statement
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Gtz.PivotHalfAttained :  every boundary (5,3) system has an atom with
                         rho_a <= 1/2.
Together with the landed floor this says min_a rho_a = 1/2 exactly, and an atom
attaining it is avoided by all four triples of its complement -- four dominating

Equivalent primal forms of "rho_a <= 1/2", all verified:
    rho_a <= 1/2  <=>  W >= 2 g_a g_a^T  <=>  sum_{c/=a} g_c g_c^T >= I + g_a g_a^T
                  <=>  Sigma - I - 2 g_a g_a^T  psd,   Sigma = sum_c g_c g_c^T.
In the whitened co-design (ghat_c = 2 W^{-1/2} g_c, tau_c = (1-t_c)/4, a genuine
weighted (5,3) design with lhat_c = 4 rho_c) the floor reads lhat_a >= 2 and the
target reads min_a lhat_a = 2, with sum_a tau_a lhat_a = 3 and lhat_a <= 4
whenever some dominating triple avoids a.

WHY AVERAGING CANNOT DO IT: sum_a (1-t_a) rho_a = 3 with sum_a (1-t_a) = 4, so
the co-weighted mean of rho is 3/4, ABOVE 1/2.  The target asks one atom to sit
below the mean by a definite amount; it must consume the boundary hypothesis,
not just the moments.

ROUTE THAT WORKS ON PAPER BUT IS NOT FORMALISED: in the rank-two chart a
boundary system is exactly a maximiser of the linear functional sum_c m_c y_c
over the region {y in [0,1)^5 : y_a y_b <= <u_a,u_b>^2}, with maximum exactly 1
(the inequality max <= 1 IS GtzWeighted 5 2).  KKT at that maximiser gives
    m_c = sum_{(c,d) tight} lambda_cd y_d,  lambda >= 0,
so every vertex has degree at least one in the tight graph -- hence at least
three tight pairs, with equality only for the graph P3 + K2.  Excluding that one
graph, or upgrading to "some vertex has degree four", closes Q7.1.  A
perturbation/KKT argument is the obstacle to formalising this.

F71-7  THE MAIN (6,3) STATEMENT WITH THE ATOMS REMOVED  -- verified 4000/4000
================================================================================
The half-floor work exposed the right chart for the WHOLE problem, not just for
Question 7.1.  Write s_c = 1 - t_c, W = subsetSum D univ - 1 (PosDef), and

    Pi a b  =  g_a . (W^-1 g_b)          Gtz.resolventReading

Then, hypothesis-free at every size and rank (Gtz.sum_coWeight_mul_resolventReading_mul):

    sum_c s_c * Pi a c * Pi c b  =  Pi a b        i.e.   Pi diag(s) Pi = Pi,

and sum_c s_c Pi c c = k (Gtz.descent_identity).  So

    P := diag(sqrt s) Pi diag(sqrt s)

is an ORTHOGONAL PROJECTION of R^m of rank exactly k, with P_cc = s_c rho_c.
This is the CO-Parseval projection.  It is NOT the campaign's landed
Gtz.projectionOfDesign (which is the Parseval one, P0_cd = sqrt(t_c t_d)<g_c,g_d>);
the two coincide EXACTLY when all weights are equal, because the co-Parseval
operator is then a multiple of the identity.

THE CRITERION (verified on 4000 triples of 200 random (6,3) designs, zero
mismatches, after checking P^2 = P and tr P = 3 on each):

    triple C dominates   <=>   Q[Cc,Cc]  >=  diag(t_d : d in Cc),   Q = I - P,

with Cc the complementary triple.  Derivation: C dominates <=> sum_{e in Cc} g_e g_e^T <= W
(the complement dictionary) <=> Pi[Cc,Cc] <= I_3 <=> P[Cc,Cc] <= diag(s)_{Cc}
<=> Q[Cc,Cc] >= diag(t)_{Cc}.

CONSEQUENCE -- GtzWeighted 6 3 is equivalent to a statement with no atoms in it:

    ** Let Q be a rank-three orthogonal projection of R^6 and let t_1..t_6 > 0
       with sum t_c = 1.  Then some three-element D has Q[D,D] >= diag(t_d). **

The correspondence is onto: given any rank-three projection P of R^6 and any
such t, set a_c = rows of A with A A^T = P, A^T A = I_3, gtilde_c = a_c/sqrt(s_c),
M = sum_c t_c gtilde_c gtilde_c^T (PosDef), and R with R^T R = M; then
g_c = R^{-T} gtilde_c is a (6,3) design whose co-Parseval projection is P.
Checked: sum_c t_c g_c g_c^T = I and Sigma - I = W both come out automatically.

At UNIFORM weights t = 1/6 the co-Parseval and Parseval projections agree and the
statement is the classical one: some k rows of a matrix with orthonormal columns
have smallest singular value at least 1/sqrt(n).  So this chart contains the
classical form and extends it to arbitrary weights with the weights appearing
ONLY as diag(t) on the right-hand side -- never inside the block.

WHY THIS IS WORTH A LANE: the statement is now pure finite-dimensional linear
algebra about principal blocks of a half-dimensional projection, which is the
natural home of restricted invertibility (Bourgain-Tzafriri) and of the
Marcus-Spielman-Srivastava interlacing method.  Nothing in the campaign's
vocabulary -- leverages, pair minors, brackets -- survives into it.
