THE NO-FIXED-POINT THEOREM
--------------------------
The brief asked for the projection-chart argument: `M_E = -N M^{-1} N` with
`M = P - diag(t)` and `N = diag(sqrt(t(1-t)))`, where a fixed point wants
`(N^{-1/2} M N^{-1/2})^2 = -1` for a real symmetric matrix.

A shorter proof is available inside the Naimark vocabulary and that is what is landed.
A fixed point makes the sharp atoms `R g_c` for an invertible `R`.  Pair the dependency
law of the dual against the whitener and DO NOT invert anything:

    sum_j W j p . (dependency at (i,j))  =  0
      gives  sum_c t_c g_c i . (W^T co_c) p = 0
      and the fixed point makes  W^T co_c = sqrt((1-t_c)/t_c) . R g_c ,
      so   sum_c sqrt(t_c (1 - t_c)) . g_c i (R g_c) p = 0 .

Undo `R` by pairing with `R^{-1}`, set the two indices equal, and the coefficient
`sqrt(t_c (1 - t_c))` is strictly positive, so every atom vanishes at that coordinate
and Parseval reads one as zero.  No chart, no inverse of `M`, no spectral theorem, and
it holds at every design, every dual and every whitening frame.

`Gtz.not_naimarkSharpAtom_eq_mulVec` is the congruence form and
`Gtz.not_naimarkSharpAtom_eq` the plain one.
