K2-1. THE WEIGHT COUNT THAT KILLS THE AVOIDING CASE (kernel)
------------------------------------------------------------
At (6,3) with l_a = 1 and g_a orthogonal to g_y, g_z, a funnel dominator T
(avoids a, fixes g_a) CANNOT avoid both y and z.  If it did, T would be the
complement of {a,y,z}, and Parseval at g_a reads

    sum_{c in T} t_c m_c = 1 - t_a = t_y + t_z + sum_{c in T} t_c ,   m_c=(g_c.g_a)^2

while the funnel budget sum_{c in T} m_c = 1 plus t_c <= sum_T t_d caps the left
side at sum_T t_c.  Two positive weights have nowhere to go.  SIZE-BOUND ON
PURPOSE: it works because the complement of {a,y,z} at (6,3) is a triple.

K2-2. THE UNIT ATOM'S ROW OF THE PAIR-MINOR BUDGET (kernel, new law)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
l_a = 1  =>  pairGapMinor(g_a, g_b) = -(g_a.g_b)^2 for every b, hence

    sum_b t_a t_b pairGapMinor(g_a,g_b) = -t_a   (exact, every design, every size)

because Parseval at g_a totals the squared readings at l_a = 1.  Against the
landed pairMinor_budget (all rows total 1) the OTHER rows carry 1 + t_a.
A unit atom is a pure debtor and its debt is exactly its weight.

K2-3. SOME ATOM OUTSIDE THE FUNNEL DOMINATOR READS ABOVE ONE (kernel)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The cap in K2-1 is STRICT (no single weight of a triple exhausts the triple's
weight sum, and some reading is positive), so the two atoms outside T carry
strictly more than their own weight, and one of them has (g_u.g_a)^2 > 1.
Consequences, both landed: l_u > 1 by Cauchy-Schwarz against the unit atom, and
pairGapMinor(g_a,g_u) < -1.  The landed predecessor of this needed the two-zero
stratum; this one needs only the funnel.

K2-4. HAND DERIVATION, NOT YET FORMALIZED — THE TWO-PLANE COLLAPSE
------------------------------------------------------------------
Configuration: (6,3) tie, no parallel pair, l_a = 1, g_a orthogonal to g_y,g_z,
funnel T = {y,d,d'} (by K2-1 exactly one of y,z is inside; take y).  Then

  (i)  m_y = 0, so m_d + m_{d'} = 1 and pairGapMinor(g_d,g_{d'}) = 0   [landed]
  (ii) g_a = m'_d g_d + m'_{d'} g_{d'}  with m'_c = g_c.g_a            [landed]
  (iii) by K2-3 the outside reader is e (NOT z, since m_z = 0), so m'_e =/= 0.

  Parseval as an operator at g_a (parseval_reproduces):
      t_a g_a + t_d m'_d g_d + t_{d'} m'_{d'} g_{d'} + t_e m'_e g_e = g_a
  substitute (ii) and collect:

      m'_d (t_a + t_d - 1) g_d + m'_{d'} (t_a + t_{d'} - 1) g_{d'}
        + t_e m'_e g_e = 0 .

  The last coefficient is nonzero, so {g_d, g_{d'}, g_e} IS LINEARLY DEPENDENT:
      tripleBracket(g_d, g_{d'}, g_e) = 0 .
  Hence FOUR atoms a, d, d', e lie in one plane P, and the whole component of
  Parseval orthogonal to P is carried by y and z alone:
      t_y (g_y.n)^2 + t_z (g_z.n)^2 = 1  for the unit normal n of P,
  with g_y, g_z in span(e2, n) because both are orthogonal to g_a in P.

K2-5. WHAT THE FIXTURES SAY (guard against the graveyard law)
--------------------------------------------------------------
