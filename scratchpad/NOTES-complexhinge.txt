ANSWER: NO.  The hinge is FALSE over C, and it is false already at rank two.

The hinge over C: every (6,3) complex tie has a pair with g_q = z g_p, z in C.

1. THE RANK-TWO PICTURE (why the field enters, exactly)

Write a rank-two atom as  g g^* = (u/2)(I + n . sigma),  u = |g|^2,  n in S^2.
Then n is the Bloch vector of the atom and
  - Parseval  <=>  sum_c w_c = 1 and sum_c w_c n_c = 0,  w_c := t_c u_c / 2
  - |<g_a,g_b>|^2 = u_a u_b (1 + n_a . n_b)/2
  - det(S_ab - 1) = (u_a - 1)(u_b - 1) - |<g_a,g_b>|^2
  - g_a parallel g_b  <=>  n_a = n_b.
A rank-two design is unitarily equivalent to a REAL one exactly when all its
Bloch vectors lie on one great circle (a plane through 0).  Reason: g g^* is
real symmetric iff n_y = 0, and SU(2) conjugation is SO(3) rotation of n.

THIS IS THE REAL INGREDIENT.  Over R the Bloch vectors are confined to a
2-plane; over C they get the third dimension.  With all u_c = 2 a pair
dominates iff n_a . n_b <= -1/2, so a tie is  min_{a<b} n_a . n_b = -1/2.
Over R (a circle) three points pairwise at 120 degrees are forced to be the
Mercedes frame, hence two of any four atoms coincide -- the parallel pair.
Over C (a sphere) the same minimum is reachable with FOUR distinct directions.
