F31-1. THE STRUCTURE THEOREM (derived here, then landed)
--------------------------------------------------------
C = {x,y,z} a weak dominator, gap corank one, unit null w, beta_c = g_c.w,
Sum beta_c^2 = 1.  K1 := exactly one zero, say beta_x = 0.  Then:

  w = beta_y g_y + beta_z g_z          (resolve identity, beta_x = 0)
  => beta_y u_y + beta_z u_z = 0       (u = component orthogonal to w)
  => u_y ANTI-PARALLEL u_z
  => g_y, g_z lie in ONE PLANE Pi CONTAINING w, and g_x _|_ w.

  (S1) S_{yz} w = w                    -- THE PAIR ALONE FIXES w
  (S2) (l_y-1)(l_z-1) = <g_y,g_z>^2    -- singular pair gap
  (S3) wedge_{yz} = l_y + l_z - 1
  (S4) u_y = 0 <=> g_y || g_z || w <=> rank(S_C) <= 2: NOT a dominator.
       So in a genuine K1 dominator g_y and g_z are never parallel.

NORMAL FORM: S_{yz} = w w' + A v v' with A = l_y+l_z-1 > 1, v _|_ w in Pi,
and g_x = alpha v + gamma n (n = w x v), with corank exactly one <=>
  (A-1)(gamma^2-1) > alpha^2   and   gamma^2 > 1.
Invariant form of the same:  (A-1)(l_x-1) > <g_x,g_y>^2 + <g_x,g_z>^2 ,
which is exactly e2(S_C - 1) > 0.

DIMENSION: with w = e1 and v = e2 fixed by rotation, K1 has 4 parameters
against 5 for the corank-one locus.  K1 IS CODIMENSION ONE.  A generic
corank-one sampler NEVER hits it -- K1 must be built, not found.  (K2 is
codimension two, K0 is generic.)
