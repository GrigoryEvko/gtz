2. THE EXACT IDENTITIES (checked in exact rationals, 400 random draws each)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Write a symmetric 3x3 gap in an orthonormal frame (pOne, pTwo, axis):
   M = [aa bb dd; bb cc ee; dd ee ff],  D := aa cc - bb^2.

(a) closed-form Schur expansion
       det M = D * ff - (aa ee^2 - 2 bb dd ee + cc dd^2)

(b) matrix-determinant lemma at 2x2, for a third atom with frame coordinates (x,y,z)
       D_new = D + alpha,   alpha := cc x^2 - 2 bb x y + aa y^2

(c) THE RANK-ONE SCHUR UPDATE, cleared of denominators
       D * det(M + atom) = det M * D_new + (z D - beta)^2
       beta := x (cc dd - bb ee) + y (aa ee - bb dd)

(c) is the lane's centre.  It says the third atom's contribution to the Schur
complement is an EXACT SQUARE.  In quotient form:
       Schur(a,b,c) = Schur(a,b) + (z - beta/D)^2 / (1 + alpha/D)
which is the Sherman-Morrison collapse:  1 - alpha + alpha^2/(1+alpha) = 1/(1+alpha).

3. THE TIE CONSTRAINT
---------------------
At a tie with a plane-strict pair {a,b}:  D > 0 (plane block PD), det M < 0
(the pair never weakly dominates at rank three), det(M + atom) <= 0 (tree gate).
Feed (c):
       (z_c D - beta_c)^2  <=  (- det M) * (D + alpha_c)      for EVERY third atom c
                                                              and EVERY plane-strict axis.
A two-parameter family of scalar inequalities on the sphere of normals.

4. THE PARSEVAL AGGREGATE
-------------------------
Put w := D * axis - (cc dd - bb ee) * pOne - (aa ee - bb dd) * pTwo.  Then
       g_c . w = z_c D - beta_c        (an identity, no hypotheses)
       w . w   = D^2 + (cc dd - bb ee)^2 + (aa ee - bb dd)^2
       sum_c t_c alpha_c = aa + cc     (polarized Parseval on the plane)
       sum_c t_c (g_c . w)^2 = w . w   (Parseval at the probe w)
Summing the tie constraint over the complement of the pair:
       w . w  <=  (- det M)(D + aa + cc) + t_a (g_a.w)^2 + t_b (g_b.w)^2.

5. THE GENERICITY-FREE PLANE-STRICT PAIR
----------------------------------------
Shadow leverage:  l^Pi_c = l_c - (g_c . axis)^2.
Feed the tree's Nesterenko engine to the shadow design:
       1 + t_d < 2 t_d (l_d - (g_d . axis)^2)   =>   a plane-strict pair through d.
No transversality, no four labels, no moment curve.  Taking the axis orthogonal
to g_d gives the clean corollary
       1 + t_d < 2 t_d l_d   =>   some plane carries a plane-strict pair through d,
and the hypothesis is an OPEN BAND of normals, not one circle:
       (g_d . axis)^2 < l_d - 1/2 - 1/(2 t_d).

8. THE FRAME LEAVES: THE PAIR-NORMAL FACTORIZATION  (added after the first build)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Put the axis along the pair's OWN normal.  Then g_a . v = g_b . v = 0, so
   plane block determinant D0 = pairGapMinor(a,b) = P
   Schur probe w = adjugate(S_ab - 1) . v = P v
   det(S_ab - 1) = -P                    (a 3x3 determinant identity, checked exactly)
   det(S_abc - 1) = tripleGapDet(a,b,c)  (a 3x3 determinant identity, checked exactly)
and the rank-one Schur update of 2(c) collapses to a POLYNOMIAL IDENTITY in the six
pair readings of the triple:

   crossNormSq(a,b) * tripleGapDet(a,b,c)
      = pairGapMinor(a,b) * (tripleBracket(a,b,c)^2 - crossNormSq(a,b))
        - pairNormalDefect(a,b,c)

   pairNormalDefect(a,b,c)
      := (l_a + l_b - 1)(crossNormSq(a,b) l_c - tripleBracket^2)
         - crossNormSq(a,b)((a.c)^2 + (b.c)^2)
       = (l_a s_bc - s_ab s_ac)^2 + (s_ab s_bc - l_b s_ac)^2 + tripleBracket^2
         - crossNormSq(a,b) l_c

Expanded, the squared volume enters LINEARLY with coefficient P - 1:

   N * tripleGapDet = (P - 1) B^2 - N P + N l_c - (l_a s_bc - s_ab s_ac)^2
                                             - (s_ab s_bc - l_b s_ac)^2

The defect is the plane adjugate form of the pair read at the third atom, cleared
by N.  As a binary quadratic in (s_ac, s_bc) its outer coefficients are
l_b(l_b - 1) + s_ab^2 and l_a(l_a - 1) + s_ab^2, and its discriminant is exactly
- N * P.  So the defect is NONNEGATIVE whenever both leverages are at least one
and the pair minor is nonnegative.  All four facts were checked in exact
rationals over 400 random draws (scratchpad/planeshadow/{sylvester.jl,defect.jl}).

9. WHAT THIS BUYS THE (6,3) PROGRAMME
--------------------------------------
* PLANE STRICTNESS IS ADMISSIBILITY.  det(S_ab - 1) < 0 at a plane-strict pair,
  and det(S_ab - 1) = - pairGapMinor, so a plane-strict pair is an edge of the
  admissible-pair graph of Gtz/Wave/AdmissiblePairGraph.lean.  It also forces both
  its atoms strictly heavy, from the plane block's positive trace.
* AN ADMISSIBLE HEAVY PAIR EXISTS AT EVERY SIMPLE DESIGN with four labels, by the
  tree's moment-curve axis.  It also exists through any atom of Nesterenko excess,
  with no simplicity hypothesis at all.
* THE VOLUME WINDOW.  At a tie the gate tripleGapDet <= 0 becomes a LINEAR
  inequality in the squared volume whose direction is decided by pairGapMinor
  against ONE: a ceiling above one, a floor below one, and at exactly one the
  volume leaves the statement.  That threshold is new.
* THE PAIR-NORMAL CEILING crossNormSq * tripleGapDet <= pairGapMinor *
  (tripleBracket^2 - crossNormSq) recovers the tree's height law
  crossNormSq < tripleBracket^2 at every strict dominator, from the factorization
  rather than from the normal probe.

10. THE SYLVESTER MOMENT LADDER  (the lane's largest result)
------------------------------------------------------------
The first two Sylvester minors already have exact weighted averages in the tree:
   1st:  sum_c t_c (l_c - 1) = k - 1                        (Parseval's trace)
   2nd:  sum_c t_c pairGapMinor(a, c) = l_a - 2              (Gtz.sum_weight_mul_pairGapForm, k = 3)
The THIRD is new:
   3rd:  sum_c t_c tripleGapDet(a, b, c) = 2 - l_a - l_b
Five Parseval readings spend the design: the trace, the two squared readings
against the pair, the mixed reading, and the weight total.  Checked exactly at the
diamond (5.3e-15) and on random rank-three designs of sizes 4 to 9 (3.3e-11).

The two terms the pair keeps are pure ring identities:
   tripleGapDet(a, b, a) = -2 N + 2 l_a + l_b - 1
   tripleGapDet(a, b, b) = -2 N +   l_a + 2 l_b - 1,   N := crossNormSq(a, b)

11. THE AREA CEILING AT A TIE, AND ITS TWO ATTAINING FIXTURES
--------------------------------------------------------------
At a tie no triple dominates strictly, so through a HEAVY ADMISSIBLE pair every
third minor is nonpositive (pair-vocabulary criterion, no plane needed).  The
moment law then gives, at every size:

   2 (t_a + t_b) N  <=  l_a + l_b - 2 + t_a(2 l_a + l_b - 1) + t_b(l_a + 2 l_b - 1)

against the admissibility floor  N > l_a + l_b - 1.  A window in four numbers.

ATTAINED, twice:
 * (4,3) tetrahedron: l = 3 everywhere, pairings -1, so N = 8 against a ceiling
   of 8 at ALL SIX pairs.  Landed in Lean as tetraDesign_crossNormSq_eq_ceiling.
 * (5,3) diamond: all ten pairs heavy and admissible, FOUR of them at N = 10
   against a ceiling of 10, six with slack 2.5.  Measured, floating point.
Equality is a rigidity locus: it forces EVERY third minor through the pair to
vanish (tripleGapDet_eq_zero_of_crossNormSq_eq_ceiling).

Floor and ceiling never contradict each other on their own: the combination
reduces to t_a(l_b - 1) + t_b(l_a - 1) < (l_a - 1) + (l_b - 1), which two weights
below one always satisfy.  So the window is a constraint, not an emptiness proof.

12. THE SIMPLICITY HYPOTHESIS LEAVES
-------------------------------------
Peel the diagonal off the SECOND moment:
   sum_{c != a} t_c pairGapMinor(a, c) = l_a - 2 - t_a + 2 t_a l_a
which is positive exactly when 2 + t_a < l_a (1 + 2 t_a), i.e. l_a > (2+t_a)/(1+2t_a).
That hands over an admissible partner, and heaviness transports to it.
At rank three sum_c t_c l_c = 3, so some atom has l >= 3, while the threshold never
passes 2.  So EVERY rank-three design carries a heavy admissible pair, at every
size, with no simplicity, no genericity, no four labels and no plane.
Checked over 60000 random rank-three designs of sizes 4 to 9: zero failures, and
the heaviest atom beats its threshold by at least 1.356.

Consequence: the window of 11 holds at EVERY rank-three tie, unconditionally, and
in particular at every (6,3) tie.

13. WHAT DID NOT CLOSE
-----------------------
The cap of 5 gets HARDER as the size grows: sum_c (2 s_c - t_c) = 5 always, so the
average of the Nesterenko excess is 5/m, exactly 1 at m = 5 and 5/6 at m = 6.  A
(6,3) tie can have every atom below the threshold, and a balanced one does.  The
shadow cap is a sub-case trigger, not a closer.  The window of 11 is a genuine
constraint on a (6,3) tie but is not self-contradictory.
