0. SCOPE CORRECTION -- I DID NOT RUN THE DECOMPOSITION I WAS ASKED FOR
=====================================================================
The directive named the object  V_C = {Parseval, sum t = 1, det(S_C - I) = 0}
and asked for its numerical irreducible decomposition.  Step 1 showed that is
the WRONG OBJECT, so I did not spend the budget on it.  Reason:

  Phi(design) := max over the 20 triples of lam_min(S_T - I).
  A tie is exactly {Phi = 0}.  GtzWeighted (m,3) asserts Phi >= 0 EVERYWHERE.
  So over R the tie locus is the MINIMUM LOCUS of a nonnegative function,
  NOT a generic level set.

V_C is a hypersurface (codim 1) in design moduli.  The tie locus is codim 6 at
(5,3) and codim 9 at (6,3) -- measured below.  Decomposing V_C would have
decomposed an object that contains the tie locus in codimension 5 resp. 8.
The tie locus is also SEMIALGEBRAIC (a min-locus), which numerical irreducible
decomposition does not address.  The right first-order object is the ACTIVE SET
and its Gordan/Fritz-John certificate, which is what I measured instead.

*** TOOL WARNING, MY OWN ERROR, CAUGHT BY RE-TEST ***
A feasibility LP returns a VERTEX of the multiplier polytope, which is
artificially sparse.  My first run reported support 6 of 8 and 8 of 12 and I
nearly banked "NOT saturated".  The multiplier polytope has dimension
(#active - rank) = 2 at (5,3) and 3 at (6,3), so vertices are sparse by
construction.  Solving max eps s.t. B^T mu = 0, sum mu = 1, mu >= eps gives
    (5,3): eps* = 0.0667 > 0     (6,3): eps* = 0.0333 > 0
=> a FULLY SUPPORTED (saturated) certificate exists at both.  ALWAYS use the
max-min interior multiplier, never a feasibility vertex, to test support.
Note the sibling's bracket masses (0.1 x4, 0.15 x4) and my max-min multiplier
(1/15 x4, 11/60 x4) are DIFFERENT POINTS OF THE SAME 2-DIM POLYTOPE.  Both are
valid Gordan certificates; neither contradicts the other.

5. THE COMPLEX TIE IS NOT THE SAME KIND OF OBJECT  (tv4)
=====================================================================
Fixture: scratchpad/ctie_g.npy + ctie_t.npy.  Hermitian Parseval residual
1.0e-14, sum t = 1.  (The .pkl is a different normalisation -- do not use it.)
Hermitian design moduli: 7m - 19; U(3) gauge dim 9 verified in the kernel to
1.05e-14.  Measured moduli tangent = 23 (real dim).

ACTIVE SET = 1 triple only, and it has CORANK 2 (gap eigenvalues 0, 0, 3.764).
lam_min is a DOUBLE eigenvalue there, so it is not differentiable; the correct
first-order object is the block map  v -> W* dS_C W  into Herm(2) = R^4.
MEASURED: that map has rank 4 -- ONTO.  A strict descent direction exists.

*** THE COMPLEX TIE IS A REGULAR POINT OF THE LEVEL SET {Phi = 0}, NOT A
    CRITICAL POINT. ***
That is exactly why the complex problem reaches Phi = -4.76e-3 while the real
slice floors at +1.485e-3: over C, Phi has no sign, so {Phi=0} is an ordinary
codim-1 level set of real dimension 23 - 1 = 22.  Over R, Phi >= 0, so the tie
locus is a min-locus of dimension 5.

    THE REALNESS GAP AS ONE NUMBER:   22   vs   5.

This is the dimension-count form of the sibling lane's Schur-Horn result
(gauge O(3) over R vs U(3) over C).  Real and complex ties are not two points
of one variety; they are critical vs regular points of two different problems.

=====================================================================
6. THE CORNER ARM HAS A FIRST-ORDER REFORMULATION  (tv5)
=====================================================================
At a corank-2 corner the descent condition is  W^T dS_C W  NEGATIVE DEFINITE
in Sym(2) = R^3 (real case; Herm(2) = R^4 in the complex case).
MEASURED over 297 random real corank-2 corners (Parseval-exact construction:
inside = M^{1/2}Q with M = I + lam u u^T, outside whitened from I - N_C):
    rank of v -> W^T dS_C W  is 3 (ONTO) in ALL 297,
    smallest singular value: min 1.18, median 1.41  -- nowhere near degenerate.
    0 of 297 corners were themselves ties (Phi > 0 always) -- consistent with
    the corner arm's emptiness claim.

READING.  A corank-1 tie must block a ONE-dimensional descent (a single
lam_min).  A corank-2 corner tie must block a THREE-dimensional descent cone.
That is a structural reason corners should be harder to sustain than corank-1
ties, i.e. WHY the corner stratum ought to be empty.
The sharp criterion is the semidefinite (Fritz John) Gordan condition: a corner
tie needs Lambda >= 0 in Sym(2), Lambda nonzero, and mu_T >= 0 with
    <Lambda, W^T dS_C(v) W> + sum_T mu_T (grad lam_T . v) = 0  for all v.
NOT PROVED: random corners are not ties, so this does not by itself close the
corner arm.  A corner tie would carry OTHER active triples (mean 2.1, max 5
near-active in the sample) which could block the 3-dim descent.  What is
established is the SHAPE of the obstruction, not its impossibility.

7. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE PLAN
=====================================================================
(a) The stratification is NOT shown redundant.  I did not establish that the
    corner / K1 / K2 strata lie in the closure of a K0 component; the min-locus
    structure makes that question different from the one posed.
(b) The hinge holds at every known (6,3) tie, with the parallel wedge frozen
    to machine zero and every other wedge free -- verified BEYOND first order by
    Newton projection (sec. 3b), out to deformations of size 0.32.  Strong positive
    evidence, and a candidate PROOF STRATEGY: show that at any (6,3) tie the
    active gradients' common kernel forces some wedge differential to vanish.
(c) The corner arm reduces to blocking a 3-dimensional descent cone; the
    corank-1 arm to blocking a 1-dimensional one.  That asymmetry is new.
(d) Realness is a dimension count: 22 (complex, regular) vs 5 (real, critical).
(e) Saturation (the sibling's open question) is CONFIRMED at both diamonds via
    the max-min multiplier, with the polytope dimensions 2 and 3.
